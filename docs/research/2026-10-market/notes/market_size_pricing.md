# Indian K-12 school ERP market: size, school counts and pricing (as of Oct 2026)

Researcher notes for the report writer. Each claim carries an inline source. **[HARD]** marks government or audited data, **[EST]** marks analyst or vendor estimates and **[INF]** marks my own inference. Nothing was taken from the UDISE+ portal directly: its state tables did not surface, so the UDISE+ figures below come from secondary analyses and news reports of it.

## 1. Market size and CAGR of Indian school management / ERP software

### Takeaway
No credible analyst firm publishes a free, India-specific K-12 school ERP market size. The available numbers are global (USD 4-19 bn, CAGR 10-18%, with the firms disagreeing by about 4x), adjacent segments, or bottom-up practitioner estimates. The most useful India figure is a bottom-up ceiling of about USD 500 M for all campus SaaS (K-12 plus higher education), which puts realistic K-12 ERP spend in the low hundreds of crores of rupees a year.

### Cited Findings
- Global school management software was USD 4.2 bn in 2024 and is projected at USD 8.5 bn by 2031, a 10.5% CAGR (2025-2031) **[EST]** — [6Wresearch](https://www.6wresearch.com/market-takeaways-view/how-big-is-the-school-management-software-market)
- MarkWide Research gives USD 18.7 bn for 2026, rising to USD 57.99 bn by 2035 at a 13.4% CAGR, and names India among the demand centres. Its stated forecast window does not match its endpoint **[EST, low reliability]** — [MarkWide](https://markwideresearch.com/global-school-management-software-market)
- Mordor Intelligence puts the global school information management system market at USD 13.57 bn in 2026, rising to USD 21.79 bn by 2031 at a 9.93% CAGR. It gives no India market size **[EST]** — [Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/school-information-management-system-market)
- Mordor also claims "India directed INR 32 billion (USD 384 million) in 2024-2025 to equip 650,000 schools with interoperable SIS and learning platforms" and INR 57 bn for state-level SIS rollouts. This is public spending on government schools, not the private ERP market, and is unverified **[EST, unverified]** — [Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/school-information-management-system-market)
- An older Market Research Future (MRFR) release projected a global USD 8.5 bn by 2025 at a 17.7% CAGR, and listed Indian vendors Foradian (Fedena) and Skolaro among the key players. - IMARC sizes India **childcare** management software (preschool, not K-12) at USD 18.4 M in 2024, projected at USD 32.7 M by 2033, a 5.9% CAGR **[EST]** — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-childcare-management-software-market)
- Grand View sizes the India **LMS** market (an adjacent category) at USD 1,056.9 M in 2024, projected at USD 4,385 M by 2030 **[EST]** — [Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/learning-management-system-market/india)

The figure covers 12+ crore students and 370K institutions. It also says 40+ horizontal vendors compete, "most companies have not scaled beyond 100 Cr" and "the median revenue scale lies between ₹20–30 Cr" **[EST, practitioner]** — [LoEstro](https://www.loestro.com/why-indias-campus-saas-startups-stall-at-%E2%82%B9100-cr-and-how-that-can-change/)
- LoEstro values Indian campus SaaS companies at 3-5x revenue for India-only firms and 5-8x for firms with global revenue, and says no large M&A has happened in the space **[EST]** — [LoEstro](https://www.loestro.com/why-indias-campus-saas-startups-stall-at-%E2%82%B9100-cr-and-how-that-can-change/)
- **Vendor revenues (proxy for market size).**
  - Teachmint's FY25 operating revenue was Rs 74 crore, up about 4.3x, and total revenue passed Rs 100 cr with other income. Revenue came from the X/X2 smart-classroom hardware and related services, and that was its only revenue source in FY25. Losses fell 57% **[HARD, MCA filings via Entrackr]** — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/teachmints-revenue-jumps-43x-in-fy25-10550884)
  - Teachmint's FY24 operating revenue was Rs 17.1 cr, total revenue was Rs 49.8 cr including Rs 32.7 cr other income, and the loss was Rs 110 cr — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/teachmint-cuts-fy24-loss-to-inr-110-cr-revenue-soars-111/)
  - Entab (CampusCare) serves more than 1,000 schools and 1.5 M students across 28 states, and has been acquired by Nelnet Business Services; deal date and value were not found. Its revenue is only modelled: Similarweb gives USD 15-25 M and Owler USD 5-25 M **[EST]** — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/nelnet-business-services-acquires-entab-to-expand-into-india/), [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/company/entab.in)

### Inferences
- **[INF]** The figures span about 4x even at global level (USD 4.2 bn from 6Wresearch against 13.6-18.7 bn from Mordor and MarkWide), and the CAGRs run from about 10% to 18%. None of these reports should be cited for India sizing.
- **[INF] Bottom-up India K-12 private ERP total addressable market (TAM):** about 3.42 lakh private unaided schools × 258 students ≈ 8.8 crore students. At the observed ₹50-200 per student per year, spend if every school paid would be about ₹440-1,760 cr a year (USD 50-210 M). If only about 20% of institutions pay (see §6), actual software spend is about ₹90-350 cr a year. That is consistent with the scale of the largest vendors: Teachmint, whose revenue is mostly hardware, and Entab, at an estimated USD 5-25 M.
- **[INF]** Teachmint, the best-known "free ERP", made all its FY25 operating revenue from hardware. The free ERP works as a customer-acquisition channel, not a revenue line.

### Gaps
- No free India-specific K-12 ERP market size from IMARC, Ken Research, 6Wresearch or KBV. Their paid reports may hold one.
- No MCA-filed revenue found for Edunext, MyClassboard, Schoollog, Fedena (Foradian) or Camu.

## 2. Private unaided schools: counts, enrolment, board share and size

### Takeaway
India has about 3.42 lakh private unaided schools (23.3% of 14.67 lakh) teaching about 8.8 crore students (38-40% of enrolment), with about 258-289 students per school (UDISE+ 2025-26). Andhra Pradesh has about 15,500-15,700 private schools (about a quarter of its roughly 61,000 schools) that enrol about 55-57% of its students, averaging about 278 students. No published AP size-band distribution was found, so the 300-1,500 band count is an estimate: roughly 4,000-6,000 schools.

### Cited Findings
- **National, UDISE+ 2025-26:**
  - 3,41,689 private unaided schools, 23.3% of 14,66,682 schools.
  - Enrolment of 8,82,19,951 (Class I-XII), 38.0%, with an average size of 258 — [EFA India, "size premium" analysis](https://educationforallinindia.com/the-size-premium-how-private-and-government-aided-schools-are-reshaping-indias-enrolment-map/)
  - The same author's other paper gives a 40.0% (39.99%) enrolment share and an average of 289. The difference is probably a different denominator (with or without pre-primary); this is not confirmed — [EFA India, "40% mark"](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/private-schools-cross-40-percent-enrolment-mark-udise-2025-26.html)
  - Average size for comparison: government-aided 311, government 118 — [EFA India](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/private-schools-cross-40-percent-enrolment-mark-udise-2025-26.html)
- **National trend:** private unaided enrolment share was 34.4% in 2023-24, 38.8% in 2024-25 and about 40% in 2025-26 **[HARD via secondary analysis]** — [EFA India](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/private-schools-cross-40-percent-enrolment-mark-udise-2025-26.html)
- **Andhra Pradesh, UDISE+ 2025-26 Table 3:**
  - Private unaided schools are 25.8% of AP schools and hold 54.9% of Class I-XII enrolment, with an average size of 278 — [EFA India, size premium](https://educationforallinindia.com/the-size-premium-how-private-and-government-aided-schools-are-reshaping-indias-enrolment-map/)
  - The companion paper gives AP private unaided enrolment as 57.4%, which conflicts with the 54.9% above — [EFA India, 40% mark](https://educationforallinindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/private-schools-cross-40-percent-enrolment-mark-udise-2025-26.html)
- **AP school counts, as claimed by YSRCP and reported by ANI and Asianet from UDISE+:**
  - Total schools were 61,317 in 2024-25 and 60,843 in 2025-26.
  - Private schools rose from 15,473 to 15,710.
  - Government schools fell from 44,886 to 44,222, and aided schools from 838 to 797.
  - Government enrolment fell from 34,93,450 to 29,06,784.
  - **[Political source; counts look consistent with the 25.8% share]** — [LatestLY/ANI](https://www.latestly.com/agency-news/india-news-andhra-pradesh-loses-664-government-schools-enrollment-drops-by-nearly-5-9-lakh-claims-ysrcp-7536622.html), [Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/india/ysrcp-claims-udise+-shows-sharp-dip-in-ap-s-govt-schools-enrollment-articleshow-rd7h942)
- **Board share:**
  - 30,415 schools nationally were affiliated to CBSE as of 13.12.2024, per a Rajya Sabha answer. The AP row was not readable — [Rajya Sabha PQ 266, 18-12-2024](https://rsdebate.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/752865/1/PQ_266_18122024_U2573_p95_p100.pdf)
  - An undated directory lists 247 "Independent" (private) CBSE schools in AP, plus 12 government schools, 14 Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas and 34 Kendriya Vidyalayas **[low reliability]** — [Zigya](https://www.zigya.com/schools/cbse+board/andhra+pradesh)
  - A school-chain blog says "197+" **[low reliability]** — [Orchids blog](https://orchidsinternationalschool.com/blog/cbse-board/how-many-cbse-schools-are-there-in-india)

### Inferences
- **[INF] AP private enrolment.** 15,710 schools × 278 ≈ 43.7 lakh students in private unaided schools. A cross-check from government enrolment (29.1 lakh ≈ 43-45% of the total, so the total is about 65-68 lakh and private about 36-38 lakh) gives a lower figure. The likely range is **about 37-44 lakh**.
- **[INF] Board mix in AP private schools.** Even on the higher directory counts, private CBSE schools are at most a few hundred out of about 15,700, roughly 2-5%. ICSE/CISCE schools are fewer still. **About 95% of AP private schools are State Board (AP SSC).** This is an estimate; the CBSE and CISCE school locators give the exact count.
- **[INF] 300-1,500 band.** With an average of 278 and a right-skewed distribution (many 100-250-student budget schools, plus a tail of large chain branches such as Narayana and Sri Chaitanya), roughly 25-35% of AP private schools likely fall in 300-1,500. That is **about 4,000-5,500 schools**, holding perhaps 50-60% of private enrolment. **This is a model estimate, not data.** UDISE+ "schools by enrolment size" tables (enrolment-band tables in the full report, or UDISE+ dashboard filters) would settle it.

### Gaps
- No UDISE+ 2024-25 or 2025-26 enrolment-size distribution was found for AP. udiseplus.gov.in's report module did not surface.
- No official AP count of CBSE or ICSE affiliated private schools; the Rajya Sabha annexure AP row was unreadable because the PDF redirected.
- The 54.9% vs 57.4% conflict for AP is unresolved.

## 3. Price points (INR) for school ERP

### Takeaway
Published Indian K-12 ERP prices cluster at **₹50-200 per student per year** (₹4-16 per student per month), with budget vendors at ₹30-60 and premium or international-curriculum vendors at USD 15-50 (₹1,250-4,200). Flat licences run ₹25k-75k a year for small schools, and legacy one-time licences ₹50k-1 lakh plus 18-25% annual maintenance (AMC). Messaging is passed through at roughly Meta's rate plus a markup.

### Cited Findings
- **Market ranges.** LoEstro says campus management "sells for $4–15/student/year in India vs. $50–200" in the US and UAE, that some customers pay up to USD 50, and that Toddle charges "approximately $25 / per student per year". Legacy ERPs "charge one-time fees of ₹50–100k" — [LoEstro](https://www.loestro.com/why-indias-campus-saas-startups-stall-at-%E2%82%B9100-cr-and-how-that-can-change/)
- **Published vendor prices:**

| Vendor | Published price | Notes and source |
|---|---|---|

| Simplex ASM ERP (Techjockey) | ₹30 per student per month (100-200 students), ₹25 (200-500), ₹20 (500-1,000), ₹15 (1,000+); starts at ₹3,000 excl. GST | Monthly labels, so about ₹180-360 per student per year; the units may be mislabelled — [Techjockey](https://techjockey.com/detail/asmerp) |

| Devryon | ₹6, ₹12 or ₹16 per student per month (about ₹72-192 a year), including apps, setup and training | Claims legacy ERPs charge "₹50–150+ per student per month", an unverified competitor claim — [Devryon pricing](https://gleeful-whisper-cloud.lovable.app/pricing) |

- **WhatsApp pass-through.** Meta moved to per-message pricing on 1 Jul 2025, and utility templates inside an open service window are free — [Meta developers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)
  - India rates reported by business solution providers (BSPs): marketing ≈ ₹0.7846, rising to ₹0.8631 from 1 Jan 2026; utility and authentication ≈ ₹0.115-0.13.   - BSP-inclusive utility pricing is quoted as high as ₹0.35 — [ChatDaddy](https://chatdaddy.tech/blog/whatsapp-business-api-india)

### Inferences
- **[INF]** For a 300-1,500-student AP school, the going rate implies **₹15,000-3,00,000 a year**, with ₹40k-1.5 lakh the modal zone at ₹100-130 per student.
- **[INF]** Utility messaging costs pennies: 1,000 students × 3 utility messages a month × 12 × ₹0.13 ≈ ₹4,700 a year. A bundled "fair use" messaging allowance is cheap to include.
- **[INF]** Setup or onboarding fees are rarely published. Vendors increasingly advertise "setup included" (Devryon, eSchoolApp), which suggests that a separate onboarding fee is a competitive disadvantage at the budget end.

### Gaps
- No published price lists were found for Teachmint (paid tiers), MyClassboard, Edunext, Schoollog, Entab, Camu, Classe365 (INR), Vidyalaya or iSchool. All are quote-based.
- No GeM tender award values for school ERP were retrieved.
- No DLT SMS per-message rates were retrieved; they are commonly ₹0.12-0.25 per SMS, but that is unverified here.

## 4. AP private school fee levels and willingness to spend on software

### Takeaway
AP's regulator tried to cap tuition at ₹10k-18k a year (2021-24), which shows that the bulk of AP private schools are budget schools. The High Court struck the caps down. Software budgets are thin: survey claims say most schools spend under 1% of budget on admin tech, and principals balk at about ₹2 lakh a year for an ERP.

### Cited Findings
- The APSERMC tuition caps for 2021-22 to 2023-24, set by GO 53 for nursery to class 10, were:

| Area | Primary | Higher classes |
|---|---|---|
| Gram panchayat | ₹10,000 | ₹12,000 |
| Municipality | ₹11,000 | ₹15,000 |
| Municipal corporation | ₹12,000 | ₹18,000 |

  Transport was capped at ₹1.60 per km — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/andhra-pradesh/education-should-not-become-business-andhra-govt-fee-structure-pvt-schools-154440), [TNM, schools cry foul](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/andhra-schools-cry-foul-over-govt-s-move-fix-ceiling-tuition-fee-154508)
- The AP High Court struck down the GOs that fixed private unaided school fees by location, as a "gross violation of statutory rules and principles of natural justice". The state may regulate fees but not fix them — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/news-updates/andhra-pradesh-high-court-strikes-down-govt-orders-fixing-fees-for-private-unaided-schools-based-on-geographical-location-188511)
- The AP regulatory commission had earlier found private schools "charging excessive fees" — [TNM](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/andhra-pradesh/pvt-schools-ap-flouting-govt-norms-charging-excessive-fees-regulatory-commission-118395)
- **Willingness to pay (secondhand surveys, unverified):**
  - "73% of Indian private schools allocate <1% of budgets to admin tech" (attributed to Ken Research, 2023).
  - "82%… view SIS as a 'check-the-box' purchase" (attributed to IDC India, 2023).
  - A principal "might happily spend ₹20 lakhs on flashy hardware" but balks at "₹2 lakhs/year for an ERP".
  - Sales cycles reach 18 months.
  - Source for all four: [LoEstro](https://www.loestro.com/why-indias-campus-saas-startups-stall-at-%E2%82%B9100-cr-and-how-that-can-change/)

### Inferences
- **[INF] Fee bands for AP private schools** (estimates; not sourced beyond the caps):

| Band | Annual tuition | Share of schools |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | ₹8k-20k | Majority, close to the old caps |
| Mid | ₹20k-60k | Not estimated |
| Premium (CBSE/ICSE chains, international) | ₹60k-2 lakh+ | Not estimated |

- **[INF] Revenue math.** A 500-student school at ₹15k average fee has about ₹75 lakh in revenue, and 1% of that is about ₹75k a year. A 1,000-student school at ₹30k has ₹3 cr, and 1% is about ₹3 lakh. So ₹100-150 per student per year is roughly **0.5-1% of fee revenue for a budget school** and 0.3-0.5% for a mid-fee school. This is consistent with the "<1% on admin tech" claim.
- **[INF]** The hardware-vs-ERP observation supports bundling with hardware or payments (see §5).

### Gaps
- No current (2024-26) AP fee survey by band; no official post-judgment AP fee order was found.
- No primary survey of software spend as a share of revenue for AP schools.

## 5. Business models

### Takeaway
There are four models in the market:
- freemium ERP subsidised by hardware (Teachmint);
- payment and fee-finance layers (TeachPay);
- per-student SaaS (most vendors);
- legacy one-time licence plus AMC.

Reseller and channel commissions are not publicly documented.

### Cited Findings
- **Teachmint.** Its listings show a "completely free" option and tiered paid plans — [Fitgap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/021746/teachmint)
  - A competitor's page describes Teachmint as "freemium with paid plans; infrastructure product pricing varies by scale" — [OpenEduCat](https://openeducat.org/fr/compare/teachmint/)
  - Teachmint said it aimed to digitise 10,000+ schools for free (TeachBharat) and partnered with Punjab on 500+ government schools — [BusinessWorld](https://businessworld.in/article/teachmint-revolutionising-the-educational-infrastructure-516727)
- **TeachPay (Apr 2023):** fee collection with "zero-cost" monthly instalments for parents, where the school gets the full fee upfront, plus auto-debit and split settlement over UPI and QR. The monetisation mechanism is not disclosed — [CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/teachmint-launches-teachpay-a-verticalized-fintech-solution-for-education/)
- **Hardware revenue.** Teachmint's FY25 revenue came entirely from X/X2 smart-classroom hardware and services — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/teachmints-revenue-jumps-43x-in-fy25-10550884)
- **Free for government institutions.** Zoho announced free Classes 2.0 licences for government-funded institutions in India — [Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/school-information-management-system-market)
- **Legacy one-time licence + AMC.** ₹50k-1 lakh one-time ([LoEstro](https://www.loestro.com/why-indias-campus-saas-startups-stall-at-%E2%82%B9100-cr-and-how-that-can-change/)) plus 18-25% AMC ([eSchoolApp](https://nu.eschoolapp.in/blogs/school-erp-pricing-india/))

### Inferences
- **[INF]** "Free ERP" competitors (Teachmint) make price-led competition at the budget end hard. A paid SaaS must win on a differentiated outcome (in SchoolOS's case, compliance and data quality ahead of UDISE+/board submissions) rather than on the feature list.
- **[INF]** Fee-collection payment margins (typical gateway MDR on cards; UPI is zero-MDR in India) and fee-instalment financing are the main non-subscription levers.

### Gaps
- No sourced figures on reseller or channel commissions, payment-margin take rates for school ERPs, or TeachPay economics.

## 6. Penetration: ERP use vs registers and Excel

### Takeaway
There is no rigorous survey. The only cited figure says about 20% of Indian institutions use paid campus SaaS. Many more probably use free tools, WhatsApp and Excel, or the state's mandatory portals.

### Cited Findings
- "Only the top 20% of India's institutions are known to use paid SaaS for campus management functions" — [LoEstro](https://www.loestro.com/why-indias-campus-saas-startups-stall-at-%E2%82%B9100-cr-and-how-that-can-change/)
- "Only 12% of state-board schools file mandatory UDISE+ reports digitally" (attributed to UDISE+ 2023). **This is dubious:** UDISE+ is an online-only system, so the claim may mean schools filing from their own systems. Treat it as unverified — [LoEstro](https://www.loestro.com/why-indias-campus-saas-startups-stall-at-%E2%82%B9100-cr-and-how-that-can-change/)
- Scale of named vendors: Entab has 1,000+ schools ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/nelnet-business-services-acquires-entab-to-expand-into-india/)) and eSchoolApp 200+ ([eSchoolApp](https://nu.eschoolapp.in/blogs/school-erp-pricing-india/))

### Inferences
- **[INF]** Applied to AP's about 15,700 private schools, the 20% figure suggests about 3,000 paying ERP users, concentrated in larger and chain schools. That leaves on the order of 12,000 AP private schools on registers, Excel, WhatsApp or free tools. The 300-1,500-student band is probably above-average in penetration.

### Gaps
- No primary penetration survey for India or AP. FICCI, KPMG, CSF and Central Square Foundation reports on school digitisation were not found in this pass.
