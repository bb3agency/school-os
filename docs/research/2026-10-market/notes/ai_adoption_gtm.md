# AI adoption in Indian schools, and go-to-market for school SaaS in Andhra Pradesh tier-2/3 towns (2024-2026)

Researcher notes, compiled 2026-10-10. About 18 web searches and fetches. Evidence quality is marked on each point:
- **[A]** is a primary or official source, or a named survey with its sample size stated.
- **[B]** is reputable news coverage or a named survey reported second-hand.
- **[C]** is vendor or consultancy marketing, an anonymous page, or something I saw only as a search snippet.

Some findings come from search-result summaries, not from full-page fetches. Two pages (YourStory JanAI, The Rodinhoods) returned HTTP 403, so the writer should check the key numbers before quoting them.

## 1. Government stance on AI in K-12 (CBSE, NEP, AP government)

### Takeaway
National policy now requires AI and computational-thinking teaching from Class 3, starting in 2026-27. The AP government is pushing hard on AI and digital tools in its own schools, through LEAP and MoUs with Microsoft, Google, NVIDIA and Canva. Both make "AI" an expected word in any school pitch. Almost all of this activity targets teaching, learning and skilling, not school administration.

### Cited Findings
- **CBSE curriculum.** CBSE launched a computational thinking and AI curriculum for Classes 3-8 from the 2026-27 session. Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan launched it on 1 April 2026 under Circular Acad-15/2026. Classes 3-5 integrate CT into existing subjects, and Classes 6-8 add foundational AI concepts. It comes with teacher handbooks and continuous, project-based assessment with no board exam. [B] — [Morung Express/PTI](https://morungexpress.com/cbse-rolls-out-curriculum-on-computational-thinking-ai-for-classes-3-8-from-2026-27); [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/students-learn-ai-computational-thinking-from-class-3-starting-2026-27-cbse-ncert-frame-curriculum/amp)
- **CBSE training theme.** The Ministry first signalled the AI-from-Class-3 policy in October 2025. A CBSE notice of 9 April 2026 made "Computational Thinking and Understanding AI" the training theme for the session. One source says many schools lag on timetable hours and teacher training. [B/C] — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/centre-introduce-ai-in-school-curriculum-from-class-3-onwards-from-2026-27-session/amp); [AACRAO Edge](https://www.aacrao.org/edge/emergent-news/cbse-introduces-ai-curriculum-for-classes-3-to-8-assigns-training-theme-for-current-session/)
- **AP-Microsoft MoU (March 2025).** Signed with the AP Skill Development Corporation in the presence of IT and Education Minister Nara Lokesh. It covers AI skilling for 2 lakh youth, AI and cloud training for 500 teachers, and vocational AI for secondary-school children. [B] — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=504898); [Organiser](https://organiser.org/2025/03/14/282197/bharat/andhra-pradesh-partners-with-microsoft-for-skill-development-training/)
- **Other AP AI deals.** AP-Google MoU, December 2024: governance and AI skilling. AP-NVIDIA MoU, June 2025: training for 10,000 students. A later batch of seven AI-focused agreements covered IBM, UNICC, NIELIT, IIT Madras, BharatGen and WISER, some for "school transformation". [B] — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news/google-partners-with-andhra-pradesh-government-to-build-a-future-ready-ai-workforce/); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!bharat/andhra-pradesh-partners-with-nvidia-to-train-10000-students-in-ai-boost-500-startups-enn25060702382); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/education/andhra-pradesh-signs-7-ai-focused-agreements-to-transform-education-skilling)
- **LEAP model.** AP launched LEAP (Learning Excellence in Andhra Pradesh) in April 2025 for the 2025-26 session. It covers a play-based curriculum, pedagogy training, an AI-driven remedial assessment programme for FLN, and a stated goal of a "world-class" system by 2029. [B] — [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/andhra-pradesh-launch-leap-model-for-education-revamp)
- **LEAP app.** This School Education Department app brings together tools, resources and school operations. Its communication module replaces paper circulars with digital notices. [C, departmental slide deck] — [Google Slides presentation](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1rc-W1JFavAbk8uMp_2gMF_SPb9b0FK2Z/htmlpresent)
- **AP-Canva deal.** Integrates Canva with the LEAP app, Google Workspace and Chromebooks, uses Training-of-Trainers, and includes "responsible use of AI tools" for teachers. [B, undated in the snippet] — [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/andhra-pradesh/ap-govt-inks-deal-with-canva-for-visual-communication-in-govt-schools-1964978)
- **Mega PTM 2.0 (10 July 2025).** The state ordered it for **government, aided, unaided** schools and junior colleges. About 74.9 lakh students, 3.3 lakh teachers and 1.5 crore parents were expected. The LEAP app was used for logistics, and teachers had to present progress cards. ### Inferences
- AP's state push means private schools must show "AI" and digital capability to parents in order to compete. That helps SchoolOS pitch an AI assistant as modernity, even though SchoolOS is admin-focused.
- State orders already reach private unaided schools (the Mega PTM, progress cards). Government-mandated reporting and events create recurring admin work that SchoolOS can make easier.
- Government-school tooling (the LEAP app) is unlikely to be offered to private schools. Watch for state mandates that require private schools to use state portals, which would create integration needs or reduce what SchoolOS is needed for.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence of a MeitY/IndiaAI programme aimed specifically at private-school administration.
- I could not confirm the details or rollout status of the AP "AI tutor" announcement.
- I found no evidence of AP state-board directives on AI for private schools.

## 2. AI features in school ERPs and what schools and staff use

### Takeaway
Indian ed-tech AI in 2025-26 is overwhelmingly teacher-facing content generation: lesson plans, quizzes, homework and worksheets, plus AI classroom hardware. I found **no confirmed launch** by a major Indian school ERP (Entab, Edunext, Fedena, MyClassboard) of a grounded, cited Q&A over school records. Admin-side AI is mostly vendor talk of parent FAQ chatbots and fee forecasting. A cited "Ask the school" assistant appears to be an open lane, but that rests on absence of evidence, not on confirmed proof.

### Cited Findings
- **Teachmint EduAI.** Generates lesson plans, lectures, homework and adaptive quizzes, with a maths solver. Teachers review the questions before assigning them. [C, listing sites] — [Software Finder](https://softwarefinder.com/lms/teachmint); [Cuspera](https://cuspera.com/products/teachmint-x-19515)
- **Teachmint revenue.** Operating revenue rose 4.3x to Rs 74 crore in FY25, driven by AI smart-classroom hardware (X and X2; the X2 has an on-device NPU). A free plan from April 2025 includes the EduAI assistant. [B] — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/teachmints-revenue-jumps-43x-in-fy25-10550884)
- **Teachmint training offer.** A "One-Day AI Transformation" programme for classrooms, announced December 2024 for Bengaluru, Delhi NCR, Mumbai and Kolkata. Tier-2 AP is not mentioned. [B, press release] — [CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/press-release/teachmint-launches-one-day-ai-transformation-for-classrooms-in-schools-colleges-and-institutes/)
- **AI report-card comments.** I could not confirm this for Teachmint. Comparable features exist abroad, for example Edsby's Comment Assistant. [C] — [Edsby](https://www.edsby.com/?p=68587)
- **Generic ERP AI pitch.** 24/7 chatbots for admissions, fees and homework questions. Streebo sells a gen-AI "digital front desk" across web, WhatsApp, SMS and voice. Schools Geek lists fee forecasting and at-risk student detection. [C, vendor marketing] — [Weblink blog, June 2025](https://www.weblink.in/blog/the-role-of-ai-in-modern-school-erp-solutions); [Streebo](https://www.streebo.com/school-customer-service-ai-agent); [Wellfound: Schools Geek](https://wellfound.com/company/schoolsgeek-erp)
- **Chain in-house AI.** Sri Chaitanya's ed-tech arm, Infinity Learn, built an AI platform, IL VISTA (about USD 1M invested), and partnered with Google Cloud India in December 2024. It is student and test-prep oriented, not school admin. [B/C] — [IndiaAI](https://indiaai.gov.in/article/importance-of-ai-powered-education-in-india); [CXO Digital Pulse](https://cxodigitalpulse.com/?p=11319)

### Inferences
- Rivals will market AI on the teacher-content side. SchoolOS should position "Ask the school" as **office and principal productivity with proof (citations)**, not compete with free lesson-plan generators.
- The CBSE CT/AI mandate could make "teacher content AI" a commodity that schools expect for free, as Teachmint's free EduAI shows. Charging extra for generic content generation looks weak.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2025-26 AI feature announcements from Entab, Edunext, MyClassboard, Fedena or Toddle. A vendor-by-vendor check of their sites or changelogs is needed.
- I found no adoption or usage data for AI features inside ERPs (for example, share of schools using them).

## 3. Teacher and parent attitudes, concerns, willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Teachers are broadly positive, and a majority report some AI use, mainly for lesson planning and worksheets. The concerns are training, accuracy, privacy and job displacement. I found no India-specific parent survey on AI in schools. Global and broader Indian data point to privacy, hallucinations and over-reliance as the main worries. I found no Indian data on schools' willingness to pay for AI.

### Cited Findings
- **CENTA survey, August 2025.** More than 5,000 educators, with parents and students also sampled. Over 70% of teachers use AI tools, mainly for lesson planning. 67% rate their AI skills above average, but only 57% could spot a basic AI misconception. The concerns cited were job displacement (34%) and AI accuracy (23%). [B] — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news-in-brief/70-of-indian-teachers-using-ai-in-classrooms-centa-survey-reveals/); [Mediainfoline](https://www.mediainfoline.com/education/over-70-of-indian-teachers-now-use-ai-tools-but-misconceptions-persist)
- **TeamLease EdTech, 2025.** More than 6,000 educators, school and university mixed. 61.6% use AI. 87.85% want government regulation and monitoring of AI. [B] — [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/learning-technology/over-60-of-indian-educators-embrace-ai-in-classrooms-41295)
- **JanAI "AI Readiness of India's Government School Teachers", September 2026.** 797 government teachers in UP, Karnataka, Jharkhand and Maharashtra (not AP). 72% feel positive about AI, and 26% use it regularly. Uses are simplifying concepts, generating examples and activities, and preparing worksheets and assessments. Gaps are training, privacy and institutional support. The authors argue resistance is not the main barrier. [B, from a search snippet because the page returned 403] — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/government-school-teachers-ai-readiness-janai-survey)
- **Microsoft study (Indian respondents, date unconfirmed).** Over 80% worry about AI use by under-18s, citing abuse, deepfakes, scams and **AI hallucinations**. [B] — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2F65-of-indians-use-ai-more-than-double-the-global-average-microsoft-study-3400418)
- **Ipsos Education Monitor 2026, India sample (about 1,000, skewed urban and affluent).** 61% oppose banning AI tools like ChatGPT in schools. [B] — [Manifest Media](https://www.manifest-media.in/marketing/310826/72-indians-see-a-bright-future-for-young-people-highest-across-31-ma.html)
- **Parent privacy concern, global comparators.** In the US PDK poll (August 2025), nearly 7 in 10 parents opposed giving AI software access to students' grades and personal data. In the UK Nesta/YouGov survey, 73% were concerned about privacy breaches. [B, not India] — [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/americans-grow-more-skeptical-of-ai-in-k-12-schools-poll-finds/2025/08); [Tes](https://tes.com/news/revealed-parents-fears-about-artificial-intelligence-schools)
- **Regulation: DPDP Rules 2025.**
  - Anyone under 18 is a child, and verifiable parental consent is required.
  - Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads at children are barred.
  - Schedule 4 exempts schools' own processing for educational activities and child safety. That exemption reportedly does **not** extend to third-party ed-tech vendors.
  - Commentators disagree on timing: "effective November 2025" versus phased over about 18 months.

  [B, law-firm and think-tank commentary] — [ORF](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/dpdp-rules-and-the-future-of-child-data-safety); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/schools-coaching-centres-platforms-processing-childrens-data-are-you-compliant/); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/childrens-data-protection-under-indias-dpdp-rules/)

### Inferences
- Hallucination and accuracy is a top named worry, for both teachers (23% in CENTA) and the public (Microsoft). That directly supports the SchoolOS design of cited answers and "not found in school records". It should be a headline sales message ("every answer shows the record it came from").
- Privacy is a rising objection. SchoolOS's model (no Aadhaar numbers, tenant isolation, in-India hosting in Mumbai, LLM under Zero Data Retention) is a differentiator. The pitch should map it to DPDP, especially the point that the school exemption does not automatically cover vendors, so the school needs a vendor that acts as a clean processor.
- Teachers who already use free ChatGPT or Gemini for content will not pay for that. Value has to come from the school's **own** records, which generic chatbots cannot reach.

### Gaps
- I found no Indian parent survey specific to AI in schools.
- I found no AP or Telangana teacher survey.
- I found no data on school willingness to pay for AI add-ons.
- I found no reported AI incidents or data breaches at Indian school ERPs in 2024-26. I did not search deeply for these.

## 4. Most time-consuming admin and office tasks

### Takeaway
The evidence is qualitative but consistent. Repeated data entry into government portals (UDISE+, state MIS, Aadhaar-linked databases), constantly changing data requests over WhatsApp, and the lack of dedicated admin staff are the main burdens. I found no rigorous time-use survey of private-school clerks or principals.

### Cited Findings
- **Teacher burden (interviews).** Teachers describe about 12-hour days and constant WhatsApp availability for urgent data updates (mid-day meals, UDISE, admissions). Data "keeps changing until September". Schools often do not hire the admin staff they are entitled to. [B, government-school context] — [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/education/tutor-clerk-peon-cook-surveyor-unseen-travails-of-indian-teachers-153774)
- **Duplicate entry across portals.** State portals run separately from national systems, so the same data is entered several times (UDISE+, SDMIS, Aadhaar-linked databases). Small schools rely on cybercafes or block resource centres to upload. [C, advocacy and explainer blog] — [Education for All in India: MIS staff](https://educationforallinindia.com/challenges-and-responsibilities-of-mis-udise-in-charges-and-data-entry-operators-in-emis-in-india/); [Education for All in India: small schools](https://educationforallinindia.com/addressing-challenges-in-digital-data-management-for-small-and-single-teacher-schools-in-india/)
- **Non-teaching time cap.** A recommendation that teachers spend no more than 30% of their time on non-teaching activities. One proposed remedy is to shift record-keeping to admin staff or outsourced parties. [B] — [Education World](https://educationworld.in/delhi-timely-warning/)
- **AP private-school pain points (2020).** Managements raised fee collection, the transfer certificate (TC) rule for changing schools, and fee structures with the government. TCs and fees are long-standing friction points. [B, dated] — [Deccan Chronicle 2020](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/nation/in-other-news/011120/private-schools-miffed.html)
- **AP fee commission.** APSERMC inspects private schools, caps fees and may grade institutions. Its surprise inspections of 130 schools found norm violations, which adds compliance and documentation pressure. [B, some items undated or pre-2024] — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/andhra-pradesh/pvt-schools-ap-flouting-govt-norms-charging-excessive-fees-regulatory-commission-118395)

### Inferences
- The SchoolOS core of "enter once, check against UDISE+, the admission register and the board" fits the strongest qualitative pain: duplicate portal entry and late-changing data.
- APSERMC fee and inspection pressure supports "Ask the school" queries such as fee structures and staff records, and audit-ready documents. Private schools facing inspections may value cited answers drawn from their own records.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative clerk or principal time-use survey for private schools.
- I found no evidence ranking certificates/TCs, fee reconciliation, board registration, circulars and payroll against each other. Primary interviews with 10-20 AP school offices are recommended.

## 5. Buyer roles, decision process, budget cycles

### Takeaway
At low- and mid-fee private schools the owner (correspondent) or principal decides. Trustees approve larger deals. Sales cycles reportedly run 4-9 months, and buying clusters before the academic year, which starts in June in AP. The pricing benchmarks found are consultancy estimates.

### Cited Findings
- **RAYSolute GTM playbook (September 2026, consultancy, no external sources cited).**
  - Decision chain: teacher (user) → department head → principal (gatekeeper) → trustee or director (approves deals of INR 10 lakh or more).
  - In lower-fee schools the principal or owner signs.
  - Sales cycle: 4-9 months; premium schools 4-6 months, budget schools 6-9.
  - Pilot stage: 1-3 months.
  - Calendar: February-March, budgets and evaluation; April-May, decisions; June-July, implementation and training; August-March, budgets locked. The page also suggests closing pilots in January-March, which conflicts with the locked-budget point.

  [C] — [RAYSolute](https://www.raysolute.com/edtech-india-go-to-market-schools.html)
- **Pricing benchmarks (same source).**
  - Budget schools: INR 100-300 per student per year.
  - Mid-market: INR 200-500 per student per year, or INR 2-10 lakh flat.
  - Multi-year discounts: 15-20%.
  - Pilots: INR 5,000-25,000, credited to the first year.
  - Free pilots reportedly convert about 40%, paid pilots 60-70%.
  - Teachers use only 3-5 core features. Lacking a local-language UI is a tier-2 mistake.

  [C] — [RAYSolute](https://www.raysolute.com/edtech-india-go-to-market-schools.html)
- **Field sales workflow.** Identify schools, cold call, demo on premises, then meet senior management to close. [C, job listing] — [GoHire: Shri Educare ERP sales](https://jobs.gohire.io/shri-educare-limited-o9lf3xun/erp-sales-shri-educare-limited-17858)
- **Getting meetings.** A founder's account says getting a first appointment with principals became very hard within months. I saw only a snippet because the page returned 403. [C] — [The Rodinhoods](https://www.therodinhoods.com/post/13-untold-truths-of-selling-to-schools-in-india/?amp=1)

### Inferences
- For AP tier-2/3 independent schools, the economic buyer is almost certainly the owner or correspondent, often also the founder. The principal is the influencer and gatekeeper, and the clerk or accountant is the daily user who can veto through non-use.
- Target the February-May window for closing, before June reopening. June-July is for onboarding (spreadsheet import), and admissions season data entry is the moment of highest pain.
- Fees in AP are commonly collected in instalments, so expect requests for term-wise or instalment payment. This is an inference, not a sourced finding.

### Gaps
- I found no AP-specific evidence on correspondent versus principal roles, payment terms (annual or instalments), or GST and invoicing preferences.
- I found no data on what AP schools currently pay for ERPs.

## 6. Sales channels: associations, expos, referrals, WhatsApp/YouTube, vendor partnerships

### Takeaway
Private-school associations exist in AP and Telangana and have direct access to the Education Minister. They are a plausible trust channel, but I found no evidence of them endorsing software vendors. Public evidence on channel models (resellers, publisher or uniform-vendor partnerships, Telugu YouTube or WhatsApp marketing) was essentially absent.

### Cited Findings
- **APPUSMA (Andhra Pradesh Private Un-Aided Schools' Managements' Association).** Exists and lobbies the Chief Minister, for example over the 2020 reopening. A separate "AP Private Schools Managements Association" (president K. Chandra Sekhar, 2020) claimed to represent the managements of 15,000+ private schools. [B, dated 2020] — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/ap-govt-wants-schools-reopen-sept-5-private-schools-oppose-move-131487); [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/nation/in-other-news/011120/private-schools-miffed.html)
- **Ten-year recognition.** Education Minister Nara Lokesh met several private-school associations and announced that private-school recognition would be extended to ten years. This shows the associations' policy access. [C, date unclear] — [AP7AM](https://www.ap7am.com/en/94716/minister-nara-lokesh-announces-good-news-for-private-schools-in-andhra-pradesh)
- **Telangana TRSMA.** In July 2026 it urged the government to protect budget private schools. It shows that a budget-school association is active in the neighbouring state. [B] — [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/trsma-urges-telangana-government-to-protect-budget-private-schools)
- **Unverified adoption gap.** One unverified page claims tier-2/3 "digital-first" adoption of 22% versus 61% in tier-1. It names regional-language parent apps, not price, as the biggest blocker, and calls state-board schools the fastest-growing ERP buyers. [C, anonymous page on a lovable.app domain; do not quote without verification] — [Devryon Research 2026](https://gleeful-whisper-cloud.lovable.app/research/state-of-school-erp-india-2026)

### Inferences
- Associations such as APPUSMA and district-level management associations are likely the best credibility channel. Offer association-member pricing, sessions at district meetings, and "compliance help" talks (UDISE+, DPDP, APSERMC). This is a hypothesis to test.
- With an English-first product (ADR-0036), the Telugu-language finding cuts both ways. Support and onboarding in Telugu (phone and WhatsApp) may matter even if the UI is English.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence on Didac India or World Education Summit effectiveness for AP schools.
- I found no evidence on reseller or channel-partner economics, publisher or uniform-vendor bundling, or Telugu YouTube or WhatsApp ERP marketing.
- I found no 2025-26 leadership details for APPUSMA. Telugu-language press (Eenadu, Sakshi) would likely help.

## 7. Adoption barriers and what successful vendors do

### Takeaway
The repeated barriers are staff resistance, weak support, complexity, connectivity (cybercafe uploads) and language. The fixes vendors cite are paid pilots, bundled training and implementation, and a short core feature set. Little of this evidence is independent.

### Cited Findings
- **Implementation obstacles.** Teacher and staff resistance, integration with existing student-information and payroll systems, and scarce reliable technical support. [C, vendor blog] — [ClassOn](https://classonapp.com/resources/blogs/overcoming-common-challenges-in-school-erp-implementation)
- **Vendor practice.** Vendors pitch tiered pricing for budget schools. [C] — [OpenEduCat](https://openeducat.org/blog/our-blog-1/post/education-erp-for-school-oppurtunities-challenges-and-new-players-81)
- **Connectivity.** Small schools use cybercafes or block resource centres for portal uploads. [C] — [Education for All in India](https://educationforallinindia.com/addressing-challenges-in-digital-data-management-for-small-and-single-teacher-schools-in-india/)
- **Bundling and scope.** Bundle implementation and teacher training into contracts. Teachers use only 3-5 core features. [C] — [RAYSolute](https://www.raysolute.com/edtech-india-go-to-market-schools.html)

### Inferences
- The SchoolOS spreadsheet import with a 24-hour revert, and its offline-tolerant design, answer real barriers. "We migrate your Excel for you" should be standard in the offer.
- "Fear of government scrutiny" (mismatches surfacing) was not evidenced in the sources. Position mismatch flags as "fix before the portal rejects it", which is a protective framing.

### Gaps
- I found no independent studies of ERP churn or failed implementations in AP.
- I found no evidence on bad prior vendor experiences.

## 8. Large chains versus independent schools

### Takeaway
The big AP and Telangana chains have their own ed-tech arms. Sri Chaitanya's Infinity Learn, with its own AI platform and roughly 8.5 lakh students across the group, is the clearest case. Chains are not a realistic early SchoolOS target. Independent schools are the market.

### Cited Findings
- **Sri Chaitanya.** Launched Infinity Learn in 2021-22 (sources differ on the year) with about USD 50M of internal funds, and built the AI platform IL VISTA. It launched Infinity One tutoring in January 2026. The group has 8.5 lakh+ students across AP, Telangana and other states. [B] — [The News Minute](https://thenewsminute.com/atom/sri-chaitanya-group-enters-edtech-space-infinity-learn-151242); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Chaitanya_Educational_Institutions)

### Inferences
- Chains (Sri Chaitanya, Narayana and likely others) have in-house or captive technology and procurement leverage. Independent schools and small multi-campus groups of 2-10 schools are the realistic segment for SchoolOS.

### Gaps
- I found no evidence on in-house administration or ERP systems at Narayana, Ravindra Bharathi, Bhashyam or Kakatiya.
- I could not confirm whether Infinity Learn or IL VISTA is used for school administration or parent communication in Sri Chaitanya campuses.
